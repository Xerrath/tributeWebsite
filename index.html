Why Spiderman is the Best Marvel Hero!

The Amazing Spider-Man is the greatest superhero because he represents the purest definition of heroism: choosing to do good when there is absolutely nothing to gain. Unlike heroes backed by unlimited wealth, advanced technology, or godlike status, Peter Parker is an ordinary person burdened with extraordinary responsibility. He struggles financially, loses loved ones, sacrifices relationships, and is often hated by the very people he saves. Yet, despite having every reason to walk away, he continues to protect others. What makes Spider-Man truly exceptional isn't his strength, intelligence, or abilities; it's that he could choose an easier life but refuses to abandon people who need him. His greatest superpower is his character. While other heroes demonstrate what it means to be powerful, Spider-Man demonstrates what it means to be good. The greatest hero isn't the one who can save the world, but the one who keeps choosing to save it even when the world gives him nothing in return.

The Story of Spider-Man

From a canceled comic in 1962 to the biggest movie of 2026, these are the moments that made Peter Parker the hero he is today.

1962

The Bite

Stan Lee and Steve Ditko introduced Spider-Man in Amazing Fantasy #15. Peter Parker is a shy high school student from Queens who gets bitten by a radioactive spider and gains the strength, speed, and agility of a spider.

At first he uses his powers to get rich and famous. When a burglar runs past him, he lets the man go because it isn't his problem. That same burglar later kills his Uncle Ben. Every choice Spider-Man makes after that night comes from this one lesson.

"With great power there must also come great responsibility."

Amazing Fantasy #15 (1962)

Did You Know?

Spider-Man only got his first story because Amazing Fantasy was about to be canceled. With nothing to lose, the publisher let Stan Lee try a teenage hero who was not a sidekick and had money problems. Amazing Fantasy #15 became one of Marvel's best-selling comics.

1966

If This Be My Destiny

Aunt May is dying, and the only serum that can save her is just out of reach. Spider-Man is trapped under tons of steel machinery in Doctor Octopus's flooding underwater base.

For four pages he pushes, fails, and pushes again, thinking about Uncle Ben and the aunt who needs him. Then he lifts it. It is one of the most famous sequences in comics because it shows that his real strength is that he refuses to quit.

1967

Spider-Man No More!

Tired of being called a menace, Peter throws his costume in a trash can and walks away. For a little while he gets to be a normal guy.

Then he sees people in danger and remembers Uncle Ben. He goes back for the costume. Being Spider-Man was never about wanting the job. It is about not being able to look away. This issue also introduced the Kingpin, and its trash can image was recreated in Spider-Man 2 (2004).

1973

The Night Gwen Stacy Died

The Green Goblin kidnaps Gwen Stacy and throws her from a bridge. Spider-Man catches her with his web, but when he pulls her up, she is gone.

Superheroes were not supposed to lose like this. The story is often said to have ended the Silver Age of comics, and it taught Peter the hardest part of his lesson: sometimes you do everything right and still lose someone. He keeps going anyway.

1984 to 1988

The Black Suit

Peter comes home from the Secret Wars in a new black costume that makes him stronger and never needs repairs. He finds out it is a living alien that wants to bond with him forever, so he rejects it. When it comes back for him, he drives it off with the sound of a church bell, even though it made him more powerful.

The rejected suit bonds with reporter Eddie Brock and becomes Venom, first appearing in full in Amazing Spider-Man #300. Spider-Man chose to be himself over being stronger, and that choice created one of his greatest enemies.

1987

Kraven's Last Hunt

Kraven the Hunter shoots Spider-Man, buries him alive, and puts on his costume to prove he can be a better Spider-Man.

Two weeks later, Peter claws his way out of the grave, held together by thoughts of Mary Jane. Written by J. M. DeMatteis and drawn by Mike Zeck, it is considered one of the greatest Spider-Man stories ever told.

1987

The Wedding

Peter proposes to Mary Jane Watson, and the two get married in Amazing Spider-Man Annual #21. Mary Jane knows exactly who he is and what the job costs, and she chooses him anyway.

Giving Peter a partner who could share the weight of his secret showed a hero growing up instead of staying a teenager forever.

2011 and 2018

Anyone Can Wear the Mask

Brian Michael Bendis and Sara Pichelli created Miles Morales, who first appeared in Ultimate Fallout #4 (August 2011) and took up the mask after the Ultimate Peter Parker died.

In Into the Spider-Verse, Miles stops doubting himself and takes a "leap of faith" off a skyscraper. The film won Best Animated Feature at the 91st Academy Awards. Spider-Man was never just one person. He is an idea anyone can live up to.

"With great power, there must also come great responsibility."

Aunt May, Spider-Man: No Way Home (2021)

Did You Know?

Marvel celebrated the wedding with a real-life mock ceremony at Shea Stadium in 1987, with actors as Peter and Mary Jane and Stan Lee officiating.

2004 and 2021

On the Big Screen

In Spider-Man 2 (2004), Peter uses every bit of his strength to stop a runaway subway train before it plunges into New York Harbor, and the passengers protect him when he collapses.

In No Way Home, Aunt May dies saying the words Uncle Ben made famous, three Spider-Men team up across universes, and Peter asks Doctor Strange to make the whole world forget him so the people he loves stay safe. He gave up everything, and he did it on purpose.

2026

A Brand New Day

Released on July 31, 2026, Spider-Man: Brand New Day picks up four years after the world forgot Peter Parker existed. He protects New York without anyone knowing who he is and finally learns he does not have to do everything alone.

Its promo image of Spider-Man holding up a giant steel part echoes the lift from Amazing Spider-Man #33, sixty years later. The film became the highest-grossing Spider-Man movie ever.

The Rogues Gallery

A hero is only as good as his villains, and Spider-Man has one of the greatest rogues galleries in comics.

Norman Osborn

Green Goblin

Created by Stan Lee and Steve Ditko, Norman Osborn terrorizes New York with pumpkin bombs, razor-sharp bat blades, and his Goblin Glider. He is the villain who threw Gwen Stacy from the bridge, which makes him Spider-Man's most personal enemy.

Otto Octavius

Doctor Octopus

A brilliant scientist whose mechanical harness was fused to his body in a lab accident, leaving him with four powerful tentacle arms. He led the original Sinister Six and later even became the Superior Spider-Man.

Curt Connors

The Lizard

A geneticist who lost his right arm and tested a lizard DNA serum on himself to grow it back, turning into a feral reptile instead. Connors is sometimes Spider-Man's ally, so every fight with the Lizard is also a rescue.

Adrian Toomes

Vulture

An inventive but maniacal genius who built his own flight suit. He was one of Spider-Man's very first villains and a founding member of the Sinister Six.

Flint Marko

Sandman

After an accident, he can turn his whole body into sand and reshape himself at will. He was a founding member of the Sinister Six.

Max Dillon

Electro

A power lineman who was struck by lightning and gained the ability to generate and control electricity. He calls himself the "Master of Electricity."

Quentin Beck

Mysterio

A former special effects artist and illusionist who turned to crime. His tricks and illusions make Spider-Man question what is real.

Sergei Kravinoff

Kraven the Hunter

A hunter who calls Spider-Man "the most dangerous game," a nod to the 1924 short story. His obsession ends in Kraven's Last Hunt.

Mac Gargan

Scorpion

A private investigator hired by J. Jonah Jameson to find out how Peter got his Spider-Man photos. Jameson then funded a barely tested procedure that turned him into the Scorpion.

Robots

Spider-Slayers

The robot spiders. Spencer Smythe built them with J. Jonah Jameson's money to capture and unmask Spider-Man, and his son Alistair later built a whole new generation.

Aleksei Sytsevich

Rhino

A Russian thug who underwent an experimental procedure that gave him an armored artificial skin and superhuman strength. Created by Stan Lee and John Romita Sr.

Herman Schultz

Shocker

A self-taught engineer and safecracker whose gauntlets fire compressed air and vibrational shock waves. Created by Stan Lee and John Romita Sr.

Wilson Fisk

Kingpin

Poses as a businessman and philanthropist while running New York's criminal underworld. He first appeared in Amazing Spider-Man #50 and later became Daredevil's archenemy too.

Felicia Hardy

Black Cat

The daughter of a famous cat burglar, she started as Spider-Man's adversary and became his on-off love interest and one of his most trusted allies.

Eddie Brock

Venom

The alien suit Peter rejected bonded with reporter Eddie Brock, and together they became Venom. He has endured as one of Spider-Man's most prominent villains and later became an antihero.

Cletus Kasady

Carnage

Venom's offspring bonded with serial killer Cletus Kasady. Stan Lee pushed for a villain even darker and more vicious than Venom, and Carnage was the result.

Team

Sinister Six

Doctor Octopus gathered the Vulture, Sandman, Electro, Mysterio, and Kraven to take Spider-Man down together. Six villains against one hero, and he still won.

Spider-Man's Biggest Fans

From a kid who can rewrite reality to a mercenary with a sword, plus the kids who picked up the mask, heroes across the Marvel Universe look up to Spider-Man.

Franklin Richards

The Kid Who Can Rewrite Reality

Franklin Richards is the son of Mister Fantastic and the Invisible Woman. Created by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby, he first appeared in Fantastic Four Annual #6 (1968). He is an Omega-level mutant who can reshape reality itself, and even as a kid he was one of the most powerful beings in the universe. He once created an entire pocket universe.

Spider-Man has long been known as Franklin's favorite superhero. When the Human Torch was believed dead in Fantastic Four #588, Spider-Man sat with Franklin on a rooftop and told him about losing his own Uncle Ben. A kid who can rewrite reality still needed someone who understood loss, and Spider-Man was there.

Franklin later admitted his real favorite is his mom, the Invisible Woman. Hard to argue with that. In the MCU, Franklin is born in The Fantastic Four: First Steps (2025), and as a newborn he brings his mother back to life.

Deadpool

The Merc Who Wants to Be Spidey

Deadpool was co-created by artist Rob Liefeld and writer Fabian Nicieza and first appeared in The New Mutants #98 (February 1991). Liefeld based his red and black costume and his wisecracking personality on Spider-Man and described him as "Spider-Man, except with guns and swords."

In 2016, Marvel gave the two their own series, Spider-Man/Deadpool, launched by Joe Kelly and Ed McGuinness and running for 50 issues. Spider-Man thinks Wade goes way too far, but he keeps seeing the good in him, and Deadpool keeps trying to earn his respect.

Writer Robbie Thompson summed it up: Deadpool "views Spider-Man as the gold standard of being a good guy. He admires him." When a mercenary who breaks every rule wants to be more like you, you are doing something right.

Miles Morales

The Kid Who Picked Up the Mask

In the Ultimate universe, Miles watched Spider-Man die at the hands of the Green Goblin and believed he could have helped. After his best friend Ganke suggested he take the Spider-Man name, and Gwen Stacy told him why Peter did what he did, Miles was inspired to become Spider-Man himself.

In the 2012 miniseries Spider-Men, the original Peter Parker crossed into Miles's world, met him, and gave him his blessing. Later, his own world's Peter Parker returned, watched Miles fight the Green Goblin, and called him a worthy successor.

Being a fan is one thing. Miles carried Peter's lesson so far that he became Spider-Man for a whole new generation.

Spider-Boy

The Sidekick Nobody Remembers

Bailey Briggs, created by Dan Slott and Humberto Ramos, first appeared in Spider-Man #7 (April 2023). He was a kid with spider powers who became Spider-Man's sidekick, until a villain erased him from history.

When Bailey came back, nobody remembered him, not even Spider-Man. Slott pitched him as a preteen hero "Spider-Man won't want running around," because a responsible hero would never let a ten-year-old fight crime. Bailey kept being a hero anyway.

His solo series launched in 2023, and Spider-Boy #1 was the best-selling comic of November 2023 on the ComicHub sales chart.

Still the Best Hero

Popularity is not why he is the best, but sixty years of fans keep proving the point.

Why He Stands Above the Rest

  • He is one of us. Peter worries about rent, grades, and his family. Readers have always seen themselves in his self-doubt.
  • Nobody handed him anything. He was a teenage hero with no mentor and no sidekick role. He learned to be a hero by making mistakes.
  • He loses and keeps going. Uncle Ben, Gwen Stacy, Aunt May. Every loss could have been his reason to quit.
  • He makes jokes while he saves you. The humor is how he keeps scared people calm and himself sane.
  • He chooses others over himself. He threw away a stronger suit, a normal life, and even the world's memory of him.
